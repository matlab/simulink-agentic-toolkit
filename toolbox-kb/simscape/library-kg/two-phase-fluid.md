# Two phase fluid

Use these blocks for two phase fluid.

## Recommended Blocks

| Block | ReferenceBlock | Since | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Absolute Reference (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Absolute Reference (2P) | R2023a+ | This block represents the absolute reference in a two-phase fluid network where pressure and specific internal energy are equal to zero. |
| Cap (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Cap (2P) | R2023a+ | This block represents a terminus in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the cap. This block can optionally be used to assign a beginning value and priority to the pressure and specific internal energy of the connected node. |
| Constant Volume Chamber (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Constant Volume Chamber (2P) | R2023a+ | This block models mass and energy storage in a two-phase fluid network. The chamber contains a constant volume of fluid. Pressure and specific internal energy vary dynamically based on the net mass and energy accumulation in the volume. Ports A, B, C, and D are the two-phase fluid conserving port associated with the chamber inlets. Port H is the thermal conserving port associated with the thermal mass of the fluid volume. |
| Flow Resistance (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Flow Resistance (2P) | R2023a+ | This block represents a generic pressure loss in a two-phase fluid network. The drop in pressure is proportional to the square of the mass flow rate and to the specific volume. The proportionality constant is determined from the specified nominal condition. Set Nominal specific volume to zero to omit the density dependence. |
| Infinite Flow Resistance (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Infinite Flow Resistance (2P) | R2023a+ | This block represents a break in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the break. However, the fluid properties, specified by the Two-Phase Fluid Properties (2P) block connected to the circuit, are the same on both sides of the break. This block can optionally be used to assign a beginning value and priority to the differences in pressure and specific internal energy of port A relative to port B. |
| Local Restriction (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Local Restriction (2P) | R2023a+ | This block models the pressure loss due to a flow area restriction such as a valve or an orifice in a two-phase fluid network. There is no heat exchange with the environment. The restriction area can be optionally set by the physical signal port AR. The input is limited by the minimum and maximum restriction area. |
| Pipe (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Pipe (2P) | R2023a+ | This block models pipe flow dynamics in a two-phase fluid network due to viscous friction losses and convective heat transfer with the pipe wall. You can optionally include the effects of fluid inertia. The pipe contains a constant volume of fluid. Pressure and specific internal energy vary dynamically based on the net mass and energy accumulation in the volume. Selecting Enable fluid inertia causes the fluid to resist acceleration. Ports A and B are the two-phase fluid conserving ports associated with the pipe inlet and outlet. Port H is the thermal conserving port associated with the pipe wall. |
| Reservoir (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Elements/Reservoir (2P) | R2023a+ | This block sets boundary conditions in a two-phase fluid network. The volume of fluid inside the reservoir is assumed infinite. Therefore, the flow is assumed quasi-steady. Fluid leaves the reservoir at the reservoir pressure and specific internal energy. Fluid enters the reservoir at the reservoir pressure, but the specific internal energy is determined by the two-phase fluid network upstream. The reservoir pressure, condensing temperature, or evaporating temperature is optionally set by the physical signal port P, Tc, or Te, respectively. The reservoir temperature, vapor quality, vapor void fraction, specific enthalpy, specific internal energy, subcooling, or superheating is optionally set by the physical signal port T, X, a, H, U, SC, or SH, respectively. |
| Flow Rate Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Flow Rate Sensor (2P) | R2023a+ | This block measures mass, volumetric, and energy flow rates in a two-phase fluid network. There is no change in pressure or specific internal energy across the sensor. The physical signal ports M [kg/s], V [m^3/s], and φ [kW] report the mass flow rate, the volumetric flow rate, and the energy flow rate, respectively, through the sensor. The positive flow direction is from port A to port B. |
| Pressure, Temperature & Internal Energy Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Pressure, Temperature & Internal Energy Sensor (2P) | R2023a+ | This block measures pressure and specific internal energy in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the sensor. The physical signal ports P, Pa, and Pg [MPa] report the pressure difference across the sensor, the absolute pressure at port A, and the gauge pressure at port A, respectively. The physical signal ports T [K] and U [kJ/kg] report the temperature and specific internal energy, respectively at port A or across the sensor. The measurements are positive when the values at port A are greater than the values at port B. |
| Saturation Properties Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Saturation Properties Sensor (2P) | R2023a+ | This block measures saturated thermodynamic fluid states based on the pressure or temperature in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the sensor. The physical signal ports TL and TV [K], UL and UV [kJ/kg], HL and HV [kJ/kg], VL and VV [m^3/kg], and rhoL and rhoV [kg/m^3] report the saturated liquid and saturated vapor temperature, specific internal energy, specific enthalpy, specific volume, and density, respectively, based on the pressure measured at port A. The physical signal ports PL and PV [MPa] report the saturated liquid and saturated vapor pressure, respectively, based on the temperature measured at port A. |
| Thermodynamic Properties Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Thermodynamic Properties Sensor (2P) | R2023a+ | This block measures thermodynamic fluid states in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the sensor. The physical signal ports T [K], h [kJ/kg], ρ [kg/m^3], v [m^3/kg], and s [kJ/(kg*K)] report the temperature, specific enthalpy, density, specific volume, and specific entropy, respectively, measured at port A. |
| Transport Properties Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Transport Properties Sensor (2P) | R2026a+ | This block measures transport properties in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the sensor. The physical signal ports μ, ν, k, and Pr report the dynamic viscosity, kinematic viscosity, thermal conductivity, and Prandtl number, respectively, measured at port A. |
| Vapor Quality Sensor (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Sensors/Vapor Quality Sensor (2P) | R2023a+ | This block measures the mixture vapor fraction in a two-phase fluid network. There is no mass or energy flow through the sensor. The physical signal port X reports the vapor quality or the vapor void fraction measured at port A. When pressure is greater than or equal to the critical pressure, vapor quality or vapor void fraction is undefined and port X reports a value of -1. |
| Two-Phase Fluid Properties (2P) | fl_lib/Two-Phase Fluid/Utilities/Two-Phase Fluid Properties (2P) | R2023a+ | This block provides fluid properties to the connected two-phase fluid network. The fluid property tables are two-dimensional arrays in which the rows correspond to the Normalized liquid internal energy vector or the Normalized vapor internal energy vector and the columns correspond to the Pressure vector. The Saturated liquid specific internal energy vector and the Saturated vapor specific internal energy vector are one-dimensional arrays with the same length as the Pressure vector. If the Pressure vector extends above the Critical pressure, the corresponding elements of the Saturated liquid specific internal energy vector and of the Saturated vapor specific internal energy vector can represent any curve or line extending above the critical point. Use the <b>Fluid properties</b> button in the <b>Plots</b> section to visualize the specified fluid properties as a function of pressure and specific internal energy or specific enthalpy for the connected two-phase fluid network. |
